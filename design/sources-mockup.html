Sources

The page that manages the browser's tree — the groups, sites and links its side rail shows, in the order it shows them — and everything kept about a site: its homepage, its recipe, the browser data it left behind (cookies, storage, cache), and later its saved logins. Opening links stays where it is, in the browser's rail. Two ways to look at it: the Tree (organize) and Links (clean up). Click around: switch modes, pick a site, tick several, open the clear-data dialog.

Sources
  • Tree = the browser's rail, as a list. Groups and the sites in them, in the order you drag them into — the rail shows exactly that order (today it sorts sites by title count). A site goes to another group by dragging it there; "No group" behaves as it does in the rail. A row never unfolds: a site with a hundred links would bury the list. Clicking a site opens its panel.
  • Several at once. A tick appears on hover; Ctrl/Shift-click extends the selection. The band above the list becomes the bulk bar: move to a group, hide from / show in the browser, clear their browser data, remove.
  • The site panel has tabs. Settings: an editable homepage (the rail opens it), the group, "Show in the browser" (today's freeze, said plainly), the recipe, and the site's browser data. Links: built for a hundred and more — a search over this site's links, sorted by the rail's order (drag to reorder), by name or by when saved, a Duplicates filter, names and addresses edited in place, several ticked and deleted at once. Logins comes with the password manager.
  • Browser data, per site. Cookies are counted and can be cleared alone (signs you out); "Clear site data…" also takes site storage (local storage, IndexedDB, service workers) and the cached pages and images of that site only (Electron's per-origin clearData). Storage and cache are cleared per site but not measured per site — Chromium gives no per-site size, so none is shown. Removing a source offers to clear its data too — what today stays in the browser's profile after a site is removed.
  • Links mode is the clean-up table: every link of every site, sortable by name, site or when it was added; duplicates (the same address saved twice, anywhere) are marked and can be shown alone; names and addresses edit in place; several delete at once.
  • New on disk: a homepage per site, the order of sites inside a group, and when each link was saved (links saved before this read as "earlier"). All in sources.json, which takes a schema bump.
