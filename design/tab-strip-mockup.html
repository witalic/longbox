Tab strip

The app's two strips — its own tabs and the browser's — behave the way Chrome's does: tabs shrink to share the strip before it scrolls; tabs closed one after another keep their width while the pointer stays on the strip, so the next × lands under it; a tab opened from a page goes next to it, after the ones it opened before; and closing one hands the strip to the tab Chrome would pick. What happened

1 · Shrinking

Tabs share the strip: each is as wide as the strip allows, up to the usual 190 px. Narrower than ~110 px an inactive tab drops its buttons (the active one keeps its ×; middle-click still closes any); narrower than ~56 px it drops its label and shows only its icon. Only when every tab is at its narrowest (36 px, the active one 62 px) does the strip scroll — as it does today, with the edge fades. Pinned tabs and the library tab keep their own width. The + sits right after the last tab, and stays at the edge once the strip scrolls.

2 · Closing in a row

A tab closed with the pointer — its ×, or a middle-click — does not give its room to the others while the pointer stays on the strip: the tabs to its right slide left at the width they had, so the next × is right under the pointer and a row of tabs closes with clicks in one place. When the pointer leaves the strip, the tabs grow back to share it. A new tab, or a change of window width, lets go at once. Closing from the keyboard or the all-tabs menu does not hold anything.

3 · Where a tab opens, and who comes next

A link opened in the background (middle-click, Ctrl+click, "Open link in new tab") goes right after the tab it came from — after the tabs that page already opened, so they stay in order. One opened and switched to goes right after its page and starts a new run. + / Ctrl+T opens at the end. The runs are forgotten when you type an address in a tab. Closing a tab moves to the first tab it opened; else to the next tab its page opened (right, then left); else to the page that opened it; else to its right neighbour.
