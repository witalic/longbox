Password manager — how logins are managed

The technical design is approved (design/password-manager.md). This is what the owner asked for on top of it: changing a password keeps the previous ones, up to three; a login is edited right in the browser window, bound to the site that is open; and a login can be deleted. One login editor does all three — in the key's popover in the browser, and in Sources → a site → Logins. The key is a button of the toolbar's, beside the star and the shield, and its popover hangs from it; ad blocking moves from the ⋯ menu to the shield's popover, in the same format (frame 8).

The key — beside the star and the shield, and its popover hangs from it

  • The key is one of the page's buttons right of the address, with the star (save this page) and the shield (ad blocking): the same size and grammar — an icon, a count beside it, the state in its colour. It is always there, so nothing shifts when a page has a form or not.
  • Its popover hangs from it as every menu of the app does: right under the toolbar, its right edge on the button's, the arrow at the button, the button lit while it is open.
  • The popover is the app's, drawn over the page by the window — a page cannot draw one or click in it. Fill puts the username and password into that page's form, top frame only.
  • The pencil edits that login right here (frame 2). Add a login for this site opens the same editor, empty, already bound to this origin.
  • Locked, the popover's first row is Unlock… (frame 7); the list of accounts shows only once unlocked — it is part of what is encrypted.

Edited right in the browser window — bound to the site that is open

  • The pencil turns the key's popover into the login editor; nothing opens elsewhere, and the page stays as it was.
  • The site is fixed: the origin the tab was on when the editor opened, shown at its head and never typed. If the tab goes to another site while the editor is open, the editor stays with its own site and says so, and what was typed is kept; a login is never moved to another site by navigating.
  • Username, password (show, copy — it leaves the clipboard after 30 s — generate), note.
  • Previous passwords, newest first, each with the day it was replaced: show, copy, use again (it becomes the password; the current one takes its place in the list) and forget one.
  • Delete at the foot, apart from Save (frame 4).

A new password — the old one is kept, up to three

  • Typed or generated, a new password is marked NEW, and the list below shows what Save will do: the current password goes to the top of the previous ones.
  • Three at most. With three already there, the oldest is shown struck through, dropped on Save — said before it happens, never after. (Here there were two, so nothing is dropped.)
  • The same happens when a sign-in shows a changed password and the owner chooses Update (frame 6), and on use again.
  • Each previous password is sealed on its own, like the current one: unlocking opens none of them.

Delete — always asked, with what goes

  • From the editor (in the browser or in Sources → Logins). The question names the account, the site and the previous passwords that go with it; Cancel is where the keyboard is.
  • There is no bin: a deleted login is gone from the vault's file at once. (A copy of the vault made before still holds it, encrypted, as it holds everything else.)
  • Forget on one previous password asks nothing — it is one row, and the editor's Cancel takes it back until Save.

Sources → a site → Logins

  • The tab the Sources view already reserves: every login saved for this site, one card each — username, origin, last used, how many previous passwords. Copy is on the closed card; the pencil opens it into the same editor as in the browser, in place.
  • A site's logins are grouped by its domain here, but each keeps its exact origin (an http and an https login are two logins), and Add login asks for it, prefilled with the site's homepage.
  • Settings → Passwords keeps what the draft gave it: the lock state, changing the master password, how many logins there are.

After a sign-in — save, or update

  • A bar of the app's, under the toolbar, never in the page. It comes after a form with a password is sent, and goes by itself on the next sign-in or when the tab leaves the site.
  • A changed password for a saved login: Update keeps the old one among the previous passwords (frame 3's rule, the oldest of three dropped). A new login: Save · Never for this site · Not now.
  • Nothing is kept unless saved; the password the page sent is held only until the bar goes.

Unlock — and the first time, a master password

  • Asked for by the first thing that needs the logins (the key, Sources → Logins, the save bar); once per app start. A dialog of the app's, over everything.
  • The first time: the master password twice, a strength estimate, at least 10 characters, and the warning — once, plainly — that a forgotten master password loses the logins.

Ad blocking — from the shield, in the same format

  • The shield opens its own popover, hanging from it like the key's: blocking on or off everywhere (the switch in its head), this site — what was blocked on the page, Pause here or Resume — the cookie banners and pop-ups, the sites it is paused on (× blocks there again), and when the lists were fetched.
  • The ⋯ menu keeps what has no button of its own: zoom, find in page, DevTools. Nothing of ad blocking is left in it.
  • Same as today otherwise: the shield's colour and count say what it does on this page; a site that is paused shows the shield grey, with no count.
