In the form — the site's logins, under the field you clicked

  • Drawn by the app, over the page — not in it. The page cannot cover it, make it see-through, move it or click it: those are the ways menus drawn inside pages by browser extensions were shown to be tricked into a click. The head says it is longbox's.
  • It opens when you click into a sign-in field, or Tab into one — a field the page marks as the username (autocomplete="username"), an email field of a sign-in form, a password field — in the page's top frame, on a site with saved logins. A field the page focuses by itself opens nothing.
  • One click on a login fills the sign-in — on a sign-in in steps, the username now and the password as its step shows (built). The page's own address first; another address of the site says which (frame 2).
  • It goes as you type in the field, press Esc, click elsewhere, or the page scrolls the field away or navigates. × closes it for this page; Manage logins… opens the key's popover.
  • A click on it in its first half second does nothing — a page cannot move its field under your pointer just as you click. A site with nothing saved gets no list: the key stays where a login is added.
